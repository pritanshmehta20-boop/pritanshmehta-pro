Five Questions That Every Media Literate Should Ask

A simple framework for thinking more critically about the media messages we consume and produce.

By Pritansh Mehta September 2026 Work in progress
UNFINISHED ARTICLE — This piece is currently a work in progress. The article is being published temporarily to preview the journal layout and reading experience.

Media Literacy refers to the ability to critically analyze and ethically disseminate media to the public and masses. It is highly dependent on how you consume and produce media in a way that contributes to the overall wellbeing of the society. Its roots can be traced back to 1920-1930s British Film Institute's campaign to teach students analytical skills about media consumption. The term “media literacy” is widely credited to John Culkin, a Jesuit priest, educator, and media scholar who worked in the 1960s to help teachers and students critically understand how media shapes culture and learning. Media literacy helps an individual to skillfully discern between what is useful and what is not from the vast pool of messages communicated by a particular form of media such that it helps. It aids an individual in identifying misinformation and promote right media consumption strongly.

Here are 5 questions that each media literate should ask:

  1. Who created this message?

    It is extremely important to identify the source of the message i.e. from where the information has come out. Is it from a person, group or institution? It is important to look at the account that communicated the message as it helps in building trust and authenticity. This is one of the fundamental steps in becoming a media literate.

  2. What is the purpose of this message?

    Next is to identify the purpose behind the communicated message. Is it to inform, educate or entertain? Is it meant to hurt the sentiments or establish a connection among two parties? Is it useful or meant to evoke a particular emotional reaction? Is it meant to persuade or sell anything or is genuinely helpful?
